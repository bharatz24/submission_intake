# DPL Manual: Coverage Rules and Structures

Source: DPLRRG Underwriting Manual v5.7.26. Check the requested coverage against these rules.
A request that the rules do not allow is a finding (usually `potentialConcerns`, or
`inconsistencies` when documents disagree) plus a condition in `recommendedNextSteps`.

## Limits of liability (§2.2, §4.1 step 4)

| Risk type / jurisdiction                             | Standard limits                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Physicians, entities, allied providers (most states) | $1M / $3M                       |
| Florida                                              | $250K / $750K                   |
| New York (on request)                                | up to $1.3M / $3.9M             |
| Long-term care exposure (most states)                | $250K / $750K maximum           |
| Long-term care exposure (Florida)                    | $100K / $300K maximum           |
| New York Assisted Living Facilities                  | $1M / $3M                       |
| NY Limited Permit Physicians                         | $1M / $3M                       |
| Midlevels                                            | $1M / $3M (state options apply) |
| Medical students / residents                         | $1M / $3M                       |

- Limits are **non-stacking** for all physicians, entities and allied providers with separate
  limits. Entities and allied providers share the primary insured's limits unless endorsed.
- When separate limits are given, the **Standard Non-Stacking Language** must appear on the
  quote, the additional insured endorsement and the declarations, and the underwriter must state
  a **total aggregate, not above $5M** (e.g. $1M / $3M; Total Aggregate $5M) (§7.1).
- Multiple claims or insureds from one incident or related acts are one claim.
- Florida BBL / gluteal fat grafting: statutory minimum limits, see §5.7 in `references/dpl-specialty-programs.md`.
- A limit increase over the prior carrier's needs a No Known Claims affidavit and is prospective
  only (§1.2). Retro-period limits may not exceed current limits (§2.5).

## Policy period (§2.3)

12 months. Insureds added midterm to a clinic policy may have their own effective date but share
the master expiration. Extensions are discretionary and extend, not reinstate, the aggregate.

## Territory (§2.4)

Rated where the provider spends **more than 50%** of their time: primary office for office-based,
hospital location for hospital-based, the location breakdown plus discretion for telemedicine.
Missing location / time-split data is a follow-up question.

## Retroactive coverage (§2.5)

Prior acts (a retro date earlier than inception) are granted only if **all** hold:

- continuous **claims-made** coverage from the proposed retro date to the effective date;
- retro-period limits do not exceed current limits;
- the prior carrier is not financially impaired or insolvent.

Never over uninsured periods or prior **occurrence** coverage. **A retro date will not be rolled
while any open claim is on the expiring policy.** A gap, an occurrence period or an open claim on
the expiring policy is a HIGH-relevance finding for a prior-acts request: the condition is to
write RDI or move the retro date to after the gap / occurrence period.

## Claims-made step factors (§2.6)

Per the rate table; blended rates for in-between steps. Quote the step from `<derived_coverage>`
rather than computing it.

## Cancellation and non-renewal (§2.7)

Insureds may cancel at any time; the request is written, with an effective date and reason; lost policy release or
original form in file; no backdating without discretion; return premium is short-rate. DPLRRG
cancels or non-renews only for specific valid reasons and must follow notification guidelines
(§4.5). Part-time status cannot be requested together with cancellation (§2.11).

## Suspended coverage (§2.8)

Disability or leave of **45+ days**: rated at **25% of the full-time rate**, applied back to the
first day of leave, until return. Leave must be continuous and **not exceed one calendar year**;
after a year coverage must be terminated and ERP offered. If the disability start date does not
align with the policy effective date, premium may be adjusted at renewal.

## Extended Reporting Period (§2.9, §8.1, §8.2)

**Basic ERP (§2.9):** needs a written request and a DPLRRG cancellation endorsement, and full
payment of the current policy.

- **Free ERP:** death (death certificate), total disability (physician letter: indefinite
  inability to practise), or permanent retirement after **5 consecutive years** insured with
  DPLRRG.
- **Paid ERP factors** on expiring rating characteristics: 1 yr 150%, 2 yr 175%, 3 yr 200%,
  4 yr 250%, unlimited 500% (PA Mcare only).

**Unlimited ERP (§8.1, from 2026-01-01):** for non-renewal, cancellation, termination or DD&R
(death, total disability, or retirement = ceasing practice in the covered specialty). Covers claims
first made and reported during the ERP for acts after the retro date and before termination.

- Eligibility: written request within the policy window (default **15 days** from termination);
  no material misrepresentation or risk change; no known unreported claims or circumstances; not
  in premium default (unless DD&R waiver); clarify individual vs entity vs split tail.
- Retro date carries forward; limits and deductibles mirror expiring (no increases); non-
  cancellable and fully earned.
- Required: ERP Request and Acknowledgment, No-Known-Loss representation, roster and last date of
  services, DD&R proof if applicable. Quote valid 30 days or to the election deadline.
- Price: **270%** of the annual premium equivalent per insured / schedule (DPL class, territory
  and ILF if the prior premium is unknown). Out-of-band pricing or DD&R concessions need approval.
- Individual tail: the individual is the Named Insured on the ERP endorsement / schedule.
- Entity tail: confirm the entity had claims-made coverage and its providers are tailing or
  continuing. Split tail: confirm who owns the tail under the contract; obtain mutual releases.
- State notes: PA Mcare participation and tail coordination; FL, NY, MI statutes of limitation /
  repose and corporate-practice issues; consent-to-settle / hammer terms must match expiring.
- Bind on signed selection and payment in full (financing only with Finance approval).
- Decline / refer triggers: see `references/dpl-eligibility-and-appetite.md`.

**Unlimited Standalone ERP (§8.2):** run-off for a policy issued by another carrier (or a separate
DPL run-off policy). All must hold: continuous claims-made coverage from the retro date; ERP
effective within 30 days of prior termination; no gap over 30 days; no unreported known losses
(open claims stay with the prior carrier); full copy of the expiring policy; acceptable class and
venue. Retro date mirrors expiring; limits equal or lower; non-cancellable, fully earned, no
midterm changes; following-form and never broader than the expiring form (the more restrictive
term applies); interrelated claims follow prior-or-pending provisions; no cover for corporate /
administrative counts unless expressly scheduled. For groups, clarify individual Standalone ERPs vs
entity run-off, and recommend entity run-off where vicarious exposure is material. Documents: expiring policy, 10 years of loss runs (or life of practice), board
actions / NPDB, signed No-Known-Loss Affidavit and Following-Form Acknowledgment, roster and last
dates of service, employer letter for departing providers, prior carrier's ERP declination if any.
Price: 270% of annual premium equivalent plus a standalone surcharge. **U/W VP approval is
required for every Standalone ERP.** Issue with Following-Form and Prior-or-Pending endorsements.

## Corporations and entities (§2.10, §4.4, §7.1–§7.3)

- Separate limits are possible for a PC, SC or LLC (employees share the entity limit). Separate
  $1M / $3M entity limit: **40%** of the manual premium of the five highest-rated providers;
  shared limits only: **5%**. §4.4 gives the corporation / partnership calculation as the five
  highest-rated premiums × **20%**, then scheduled credits / debits up to 50%. The manual states
  both; flag the discrepancy to the underwriter rather than choosing one.
- **Solo practitioners cannot get separate corporate limits**; sharing the individual's limits is
  free.
- **Co-owned entity (§7.2):** confirm ownership; confirm every co-owner and practising provider
  has their own coverage; add the entity **vicariously only** with **Endorsement #26**; collect a
  COI if the entity has its own policy. Sole owner: coverage extends automatically, no COI. Co-owner
  with an uninsured partner: vicarious only, to avoid covering the partner or the entity's own
  negligence (hiring, training, supervision).
- **Entity-only coverage (§7.3):** U.S.-domiciled legal entity; complete roster of all providers;
  the entity must not deliver care itself. Solo corp: full or shared coverage, provider must be a
  named insured. Multiple providers with 50%+ insured by DPL: shared or separate entity limits, all
  providers named or endorsed. Under 50% insured by DPL: **VL-only endorsement** on the provider's
  policy, explicitly excluding all other providers. Documents: Entity Application (legal name,
  structure, ownership, all locations, services billed under the entity); provider roster (every
  owner, employee and contractor with licence type, specialty, hours, locations, current carrier,
  retro date and limits); organisational chart if there are several sites or management layers. Required exclusions: providers not named,
  direct care by the entity or unscheduled providers, high-risk services not listed. No cover for
  non-clinical activities (e.g. billing) unless endorsed; restricted specialties need leadership
  approval.

## Certificates of insurance (§7.4) and Waiver of COI (§7.5)

Collect a current COI from the third-party entity when the insured: works at hospitals, ASCs,
telemedicine platforms, correctional facilities, or medspas / clinics they do not own; works as an
independent contractor; holds a medical directorship or supervises midlevels, standing orders or
nursing staff; has a policy exclusion or carve-out for that work; or faces vicarious exposure
through another party.

Review: entity name matches the application / agreement; valid policy period; PL and / or GL;
limits suited to the services (typically not below $1M / $3M); additional insured or shared-limit
language. Flag inadequate or expired coverage; follow up annually.

**Endorsement #51 (Waiver of COI)** defers COIs when the insured works at several non-insured
facilities and COIs from all of them are not feasible upfront, or a third party will not or cannot
give one and the exposure is minimal or contractually necessary. Review the nature of each facility. Conditions: all locations disclosed; insured attests each facility carries adequate
PL; signed Waiver of COI Request Form, facility list and attestation in file; COIs due at claim
time, or the claim is excluded. Not for high-risk specialties (surgery, pain management) without
prior approval; **Director of Underwriting approval required**.

## DDR with entity policies (§7.6)

DDR (free tail on death, disability or retirement) is for individuals. **Do not** extend DDR to an
entity that has its own policy (even if solely owned), has separate limits, will keep operating,
keep records or engage providers, or still carries vicarious exposure: a separate entity tail is
required. **May** extend only if the entity shares limits on the same policy, will cease
operations entirely, gives a written attestation (no operations, no new providers, no records or
liabilities), and premiums are paid in full with the individual DDR-eligible. Documents: written
request, proof of dissolution, confirmation no other providers practise, no-exposure attestation,
claims history for individual and entity. DDR then covers the individual and the shared-limit
entity only for services before termination.

## Part-time physicians (§2.11)

Part-time = **20 hours or less per week** on average (patient care, rounds, records, consults);
schedules may be requested from every facility. It may apply to semi-retired, teaching, disabled or
moonlighting providers. Credits are in `references/dpl-rating-reference.md`.
Not available while requesting cancellation. Midterm part-time return premium of $500 or less is not
issued midterm (endorsement only; credit at renewal).

## Locum tenens (§2.12)

Automatically covered when the locum shares the insured's limits, is limited to the same specialty
and terms, works no more than **30 continuous days or 60 total days in a rolling 12 months**, and
a CV plus one-page application is approved. 1–30 days: no charge; 31–60 days: 35% of premium.
Beyond 60 days the locum needs their own coverage.

## Miscellaneous medical entities (§2.14, §4.3)

Non-separate labs testing only the insured's patients: no charge. Separate owned labs: additional
insured at 25% of the Family Practice No Surgery rate. Freestanding urgent care, surgi-centres and
dialysis centres: per-procedure basis. Misc entity rate: FP rate at $1M / $3M for territory and
step, adjusted for limits, × 25%.

## Changes in scope (§2.15, §4.6)

Midterm changes in specialty, limits, hours or insureds are priced on the exposure difference and
carry through the five-year claims-made cycle (e.g. an OB/GYN who stops obstetrics keeps OB
premium for prior acts until full maturity). Classification follows §4.6 (`references/specialty-risk-analysis.md`).
